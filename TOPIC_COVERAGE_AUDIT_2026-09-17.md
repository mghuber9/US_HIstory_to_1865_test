## Prior Learn gaps corrected

The audit found **36 questions whose exact tested answer was not explicit in the prior Learn paragraph/Know This material**. These were concentrated in Unit 1: Indigenous Cultures details, chronology questions across all eight Unit 1 sections, and one colonial women's textile-work question. All 36 are now included in their topic paragraph and question/answer list. Cowpens already had a Learn screen, but its bank-derived concepts were not presented as a consolidated, searchable list; that discoverability gap is corrected.

## Historical and question-quality review

- No clear historical inconsistency requiring a question-bank correction was found.
- No valid question, answer, distractor, explanation, or scoring key was rewritten or removed.
- No exact duplicates were found. The paired “identify the topic” and “why it mattered” questions in Units 2-4 are intentional near-duplicates in subject, but test materially different facets and should remain.
- No topic appears disproportionately weighted by an isolated minor fact. Units 2-4 consistently use two questions per topic. Unit 1 is somewhat more varied; the largest cluster is Clovis People & Megafauna with four questions, all covering distinct course concepts.

## Representative consolidation examples

- **Cowpens:** combines the question identifying Daniel Morgan's layered-defense/controlled-retreat victory with the question about damage to Cornwallis's army and effective American tactics.
- **Articles of Confederation:** combines identification of the first national framework with the consequences of its weak central government.
- **Great Awakening:** consolidates the movement's religious characteristics, leading figures, and historical effects under one study topic.
- **Navigation Acts / mercantilism:** places regulation of colonial trade and its imperial purpose together.
- **Gettysburg:** combines the battle's identification with Pickett's Charge and the damage to Confederate offensive capacity.
